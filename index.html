Is aged care keeping up with an ageing Australia?

Australians are living longer and the population is getting older. This story follows the shift from who is ageing, to who uses home care and residential care, to where the money goes.

How to read this page. Every chart is interactive: hover for exact values, and use the legend, slider or dropdown where one appears.

Chapter 1

A population getting older

Demand for aged care starts with demographics. Four views of the same shift: over time, by age, by state, and as a share of each state's population.

Older age groups are taking a bigger share every decade

Since 1990 the 65 and over groups have grown from about 11% to about 17.5% of the population, while the youngest group has shrunk.

Hover over the chart to read each age group's share for any year.

Source: ABS estimated resident population.

The shape of the population is changing

The population pyramid shows the same story by age and sex: the top of the pyramid widens as large generations move into older age. Move the slider to compare years.

Males extend left and females extend right.

Source: ABS estimated resident population.

Ageing is not even across the country

In 2025, Tasmania has the oldest population (median age 42.5) and the Northern Territory the youngest (33.8). Tasmania rose 3.6 years since 2006, while Victoria barely moved.

Each panel highlights one state; grey lines are the others and the dashed line is Australia.

Source: ABS National, state and territory population, March 2026, Table 3.

Which states have the largest share of older residents?

The map shows the percentage of each state and territory's population aged 65 and over in 2025. Darker shades mean a larger share of older residents, which points to where demand for aged care is likely to be felt first.

Hover a state for its exact percentage.

Source: ABS population estimates.

Home Care Package use grew in every state

Between 2021 and 2025 recipients rose in every state and territory. Queensland more than doubled (+103%), while the ACT grew least (+16%).

Chapter 4

Where the money goes

Government spending shows what the system is built around, and how far it has moved toward home care.

Residential care still takes the biggest share of spending

In 2024–25, residential care took $24.2 billion (60.8%) of government aged care spending, while home care and support took $12.9 billion (32.4%). Each square is about 1% of the total.

100 squares are shared between categories using the largest remainder method.

Source: Australian Government aged care expenditure, 2024–25.

So, is care keeping up?

Demand is rising

The share of Australians aged 65 and over keeps growing, and the oldest groups are growing fastest.

Care is shifting home

Home Care Package use rose by about two thirds from 2021 to 2025, though spending is still weighted to residential care.

The picture is uneven

States differ in how old they are and how much care they use, so a national average hides local needs.

A note on the data. The line chart of aged care use over time shows approximate values read from an AIHW chart, so read it for trends, not exact counts. The charts combine figures from different organisations and years, so differences between states should be read as patterns, not precise gaps.
